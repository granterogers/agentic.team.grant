# ADR-0053 — Authorization is asked once, an ask is bound to the exact action, and a No is remembered

**Status:** accepted (Architecture Run 2). Migrations 0611 and 0612. Final semantics below, after two independent reviews; the first draft's statements that a No has no reversal, that internal writes still ask at `suggest`, and that deploy order is forgiving both ways are superseded by this text.

## Context
The Run 2 map (`docs/architecture-run-2/AUTHORITY-MAP.md`) found six independent deciders of "may this effect happen?" and six of "has it been approved?". Three of them were wrong in ways that reached Grant: a specialist's grant (`staff_may_use`) was never consulted at runtime; a No to a duty's question was forgotten and asked again; and ticking the to-do that stands for a question answered it as nobody, with the first option, without running what answering promised.

## Decision
1. `app.authorize_effect(project, tool, asked_job, staff, audience, fingerprint, job)` is the one decision boundary for a consequential effect. It calls `app.may_act` unchanged (matrix, dial, standing rows, "a person asked") and layers what `may_act` cannot know: the named specialist's grant, an outside-audience floor, the exact approval or refusal already on file, and a refusal window (`system_config` `approval.refusal_days`, 30). `mayAct()` is the only TypeScript door.
2. An ask is bound to the exact action: `blockers.action_fingerprint` is the same sha256 `job_pauses.binds_to` holds. `answerFor`'s +-10 second window and resolution-sentence parse are retired.
3. The answer is a column (`answered_option`, `answered_by`); `resolution` stays for people and nothing parses it.
4. A No is durable and specific: `decline_option` names it, the exact fingerprint (or a duty's tool + project when no fingerprint exists) is refused for the window, a later answer takes it back, a cancelled job is not a refusal, and a different action asks afresh. A duty that is refused says nothing and records no fault.
5. The to-do tick is an answer through `app.hq_answer_blocker`, as a principal the door recorded (`tasks.completed_by`), or the task is reopened and the question stays open.
6. Containment (`may_act_outside`, `claim_delivery`) stays an independent witness and is not folded in.

## Final semantics

**Order of the decision** (`app.authorize_effect`): `may_act` refuse stays refuse → a named specialist must hold the tool → **a No recorded for this very step in this job** (beats the dial, a standing row and any matrix cell, including 0612's) → an outside audience cannot proceed → a Yes recorded for exactly this step proceeds an ask → the remembered No of an earlier job (only when nothing explicit overrides it: not a standing authorization, not "you asked for this"; and only from the same kind of traffic, harness or real, so a suite's No never silences a real duty and a real No never binds a suite) → the matrix verdict.

**Internal office work proceeds at `suggest` (0612, Grant's decision).** Data, not a branch: `autonomy_policy` `suggest × level 1` is `proceed`, and `drive.ingest` moved from level 1 to 2 because it brings in material from somebody else's system and its question is deliberate. Nothing else changed: every level-2 and level-3 cell, the outside-audience floor, and the switched-off level-1 tools (`note.create`, `gmail.draft`) are as they were, and the boundary names no tool and no dial setting. Consequence: `suggest` and `act_with_review` now resolve identically (four settings, three behaviours); what `act_with_review` should add is a further decision for Grant. The floor keys on the *declared* audience of a step, so a draft prepared for a client still asks; that is the price of hanging the rule on a label the model writes, accepted because it can only ever add a question.

**Taking a No back** (`app.withdraw_refusal`, 0611; the press is *Let it ask again* on the Governance page, and the gateway action `withdraw_refusal`). An explicit, attributed state transition on the question that recorded the No (`refusal_withdrawn_at/by`). It never approves: it clears the memory, the next attempt asks, and only an answer approves; an older Yes on the same action does not speak after it. It is for a person's door only: the caller must be a principal the office knows, never a specialist or a harness name; execute is granted to `service_role` alone (not `ai_team_app`, which every job runs as) and to no `anon`/`authenticated`; nothing in `src/` calls it; the to-do tick does not. Only a No, only a resolved question, only inside its window; repeating it is harmless. A different action's No is untouched, cancelling a job is still not a refusal, and a standing authorization is a different row with a different decision (granting one is how a cross-job No is overridden; it never reopens the No).

**Exactness.** What is approved is the step, what it is for, what it depends on, the project, **who acts and who it reaches**, and, for a step with no reason, the request. A yes to a step by one specialist is not a yes to the same step by another.

**Deploy order is strict, in one direction.** Migrations 0611 and 0612 first, then the worker and the gateway. The new code has no fallback to the old policy: the old policy cannot read an answer (plan steps would re-ask every round) and a catch loose enough to recognise a missing function also swallows any undefined function inside the boundary. A worker ahead of its migration fails loudly and retries; the old worker keeps working after the migration, because every old signature it calls still exists, and the migration backfills questions in flight.

**The identity-bearing wrappers are the gateway's alone (0611).** They take the answering principal as an argument and trust it. Supabase grants `EXECUTE` on new `public` functions to `anon` and `authenticated` by default and 0607/0608 revoked only from `public`, so a holder of the anon key could have named themselves `grant`. Closed for the eight that decide who approved something. **272 of 275 security-definer functions in `public` were executable by `anon` at the time of writing** and are not swept here; that is the most urgent item of the next security run.

## Consequences
- A step that names a specialist who lacks the grant performs nothing and says why. Capabilities nobody holds (`task.extract`, `drive.ingest`) therefore refuse any step attributed to a specialist; no real plan step in production has ever named one, and granting them is Grant's decision.
- Effects still outside the boundary, by design of this run: `notebook.generate` and `office.execute_plan` (blocker-only), `notebook.add_source`, `notebook.push_document`, `office.notion_import`, `tasks.sync`, `office.provision_channels`, `google.revoke` (containment-only), and the raw sends in `telegram-webhook` and `vault-export`. The architecture is established and proven for the migrated paths (plan steps, the calendar duty, `drive.ingest`); migrating the rest is explicit follow-up.
- The bearer token is attributed as `grant` (0608's design: one machine credential, one owner): possession confers Grant's approval identity, including the power to take a No back. That is the token model, not this boundary, and is high-priority identity work for a bounded run of its own.

## What would reverse this
A second `may_act` caller in the handlers that bypasses `mayAct`; a parse of `resolution` to learn an answer; the tick resolving a blocker without `hq_answer_blocker`; `authorize_effect` gaining a path to `proceed` for an outside audience without an approval on file; a caller of `withdraw_refusal` other than the gateway; a fallback from `authorize_effect` to `may_act`; a special case for a tool or a dial setting inside the boundary.
