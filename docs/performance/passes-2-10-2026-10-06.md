## Pass 6: independent adversarial review of the causal model

The reviewer was told to assume the model is wrong. Result: **the model as the brief phrases it ("substantially driven by the product's own
standing load, chiefly the Team HQ poll readers") is not supported by the local evidence, and parts are contradicted.** The existing documents
are more careful ("trigger, not cause"; compute verdict NOT YET DETERMINABLE) and stand. What survives is narrow: the fixes cut per-call work
on a replica, which is hygiene, and nothing local connects that work to the host's episodes.

Alternatives, best first: (1) host memory and swap pressure independent of product reads (swap in use at healthy idle; persistent phases ran
with zero poll traffic; multi-second statements with zero disk reads); (2) a provider or shared-host event; (3) our own verification and
migration traffic through the Management API rather than the gateway (66,138 calls of `update public.jobs set state` annotated as Management
API); (4) the worker's standing load, better supported than the poll readers and not all fixed here (`model_order`, `defer_over_budget`, the
notification query); (5) database growth against 434 MiB RAM; (6) CPU credit throttle; (7) exporter and statement-store pollution. The poll-reader
model survives only as an amplifier (a buffer hit on a swapped-out page is a major fault: untested).

**Falsified from local files** (each now corrected in place): production's update counters "agree" (47.8 updates per job against 10 measured); 45 MB
of `jobs` TOAST (0 bytes on every seeded replica); the seed's "94 MB heap" (a total relation size); 12.4 GB of temp files a day over 31.5 days (9.6 GB
over 40.4 days, so the unexplained gap is about 2.4 GB a day, not 5); "3,000 requests is a hundred page loads" (nearer thirty). Numbers that do not
reconcile: the hit counter permits far fewer poll pages than the replica cost implies (production per-call cost is far below the replica's, or polls
were a small share of requests, or both), and three different "before" figures for `office_health` come from three replica states (pages are not a
stable cost measure; time agrees).

**Consequences for the work:** the compute verdict stays NOT YET DETERMINABLE; the stabilisation release stays a hygiene release whose effect on
the host is measured by the canary and the counters after each step, never assumed; do not claim the poll readers explain the episodes. The cheapest
experiments that would change confidence, none needing production: a counter reconciliation (what the 2.37 G hit counter permits per day); a replica
run under a 434 MiB cgroup with swap, comparing page faults and cron-heartbeat latency before and after (the only local test that couples buffer
touches to faults); a re-seed with a realistic `pinned_config` width. None was run here (no cgroup control in this sandbox; width UNKNOWN).
