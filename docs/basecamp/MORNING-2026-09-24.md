## Authentication

**What now exists.** Supabase Auth — this project's own identity system, which
had never been used (`auth.users` held zero rows) — issues a person a session.
`hq-api` verifies that session **with Supabase** and checks
`public.office_members`. Machines still present `hq.api_token`, unchanged,
compared in constant time. **ADR-0043** records the whole trace and the
reasoning.

**Is the token UX completely gone?** Yes. There is no field, no paste, no code,
no Telegram approval and no pairing anywhere in the interface. The word does
not appear on the sign-in surface. The legacy key is still *read* from storage
if an old browser has one, so none of Grant's existing devices were signed out
by this change — but nothing offers to put one there.

**What a clean browser experiences.** An email field and one button. Verified
across four states: clean, signed in, refreshed, and a new context carrying
storage (a restart). 17/17.

**Security.** Not weakened. A stranger who signs up at the identity layer gets
a perfectly valid session and the office refuses it, because *Supabase says who
proved an address and `office_members` says who the office works for*. Nothing
decodes the JWT locally — a token a function merely decoded is a token anybody
can write. A forged session is refused; asserted every run.

## Live voice — SIMPLIFIED (§4)

Pressing the top microphone used to open a strip under the project head with a
status line, a mute button, an end button and the last six turns of the
conversation. §4 forbids four of those outright. It is gone.

The microphone carries the states instead — connecting, listening, the team
talking, muted — as colour and glow **and** as its accessible name, so a screen
reader hears what a sighted person sees. Nothing about the icon's box changes,
so nothing beside it moves. Ending a call is a second press.

**Two things you should know rather than discover.**

1. **Mute is gone with the panel.** It was a button on it, and a second control
   beside the microphone would be the surface §4 forbids under another name. If
   you want it back, the smallest honest form is a long press — your call.
2. **"Grant speaking" is not one of the states.** The call layer does not report
   it. A light claiming to know who is talking when nothing tells it is a status
   light that lies, so the icon says "listening", which means your turn.

`verify:basecamp-livevoice` rewrote fifteen assertions onto the new contract and
passes 28/28 against the deployed bytes. It now compares the shape of the whole
page before the press with the shape during a live call — proved by making a
panel appear and watching three checks go red.

It also found a real defect while doing it: the poll was painting the button
captured when it was wired, and a render replaces the header, so the icon froze
on its first state and never showed the team speaking.

## Team — NOW BUILT (§16, §17)

The Team screen is where the project's team is configured. Each specialist
shows their role and the subject words the office routes on; you can edit any of
it, take somebody off the project, put somebody on, and bring somebody new in.

**Nothing here is new, which is the point.** Every control presses a function
the office already had — `set_project_staff`, `hq_staff_update`,
`hq_staff_hire`, `hq_staff_retire` — and all five were already gateway actions.
There is no frontend agent model.

**It reaches routing, and that is the test.** The subject words go to
`staff.expertise`, which is the column the router matches your request against.
`verify:basecamp-team` types a word in the editor and reads it back out of that
column, and I proved the check by stopping the page sending it and watching that
one go red. 12/12.

Two things worth knowing:

- **The roster now comes from the table that decides it.** It used to be derived
  from whoever had appeared in the project's tasks and activity — a record of
  activity wearing the name of a team, in which somebody assigned and not yet
  used was invisible on the very screen where you assign people.
- **Bringing somebody in is the one control no one has pressed.** A hire creates
  somebody who cannot be deleted, so a nightly suite would build a roster of
  ghosts. The form is checked; the press is yours.

## One thing that had been quietly wrong

**Ten suites were testing a day-old copy of the site.** `.out/live` is a mirror
of the deployed bytes and nothing refreshed it; it was from 23 September 11:51.
It surfaced as two failures in `verify:basecamp-torture` about a panel resize —
driving the same gesture against the live page showed the product doing exactly
the right thing. Refreshing the mirror turned 28/30 into 31/31 with no product
change at all. `basecamp:gate2` now refreshes it before running anything.

That was invisible because gate2 had been dying at `verify:basecamp-board`
before reaching it: that suite still opened a page menu for the Arrange entry
§13 deliberately removed. Fixing one stale assertion unblocked fifteen suites,
and three of them were stale in the same way.

It also found a real one-way door. The panel holding "Put it back the way it
was" renders only when a module is OFF the page, so anyone who dragged and
resized six modules and removed none had no way to undo any of it. The offer
now stands whenever the page differs from the shape it was given.
