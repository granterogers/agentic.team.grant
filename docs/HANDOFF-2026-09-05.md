## Decisions made without you

Each is a fork where I picked a road. None is hard to reverse.

1. **Mute 24h is global, not project-scoped.** Scoping it when we happen to know
   the project and not when we do not would make one button mean two things
   depending on our own records — and the time you would find out is the time it
   silenced less than you expected.
2. **"More detail" is recorded and not acted on.** Acting means creating work,
   and creating work is the router's decision (§5, §9). A processor that quietly
   enqueued a job would be a second, unaudited entry point into the work system.
3. **An unreviewed output is never spoken.** §16's label is printed where a
   reader can see it; audio has no margin, and a spoken caveat is the first thing
   heard and the last thing remembered accurately.
4. **A delivery failure does not fail the job.** The work is done and committed
   — that is already true — and rolling it back because a message did not send
   would discard finished work. The job's state reason says what actually
   happened instead of "delivered".
5. **A `name_taken` channel is never adopted** unless Postgres already says that
   channel belongs to that project. A project quietly posting into a stranger's
   conversation is worse than a project with no channel: the second is visible.

## The one thing that would take you ten seconds

**Send anything to [@agenticteamgrantbot](https://t.me/agenticteamgrantbot).**

Telegram forbids a bot messaging anyone who has not messaged it first, so there
is no chat id, and it cannot be configured, seeded or guessed — it appears the
moment you say anything. Until then the pager's correct behaviour is to page
nobody, which it does, and says so in every job's state reason.

Everything else is proven: the registry, the lookup, the send (against a chat
that does not exist, which exercises the whole path bar a human receiving
something), the error classification, and the deployed webhook's registration
path. One "hi" turns the last of those into an end-to-end proof on the next run.

## Three things worth knowing about the pager

1. **It will not page for routine results.** A floor of `important` sits under
   the preference check: your preferences can make it quieter, never louder.
   Anything short of urgent is sent silently — on the phone when you look,
   without the phone asking to be looked at.
2. **An interrupted Telegram send can never be reconciled.** Slack can be asked
   whether a message landed; a Telegram bot cannot read its own sent messages
   back. So an indeterminate send is parked forever and never retried. That is
   the safe direction: a page that did not arrive is a page you did not get, and
   a page sent twice at 3am is worse.
3. **Blocking the bot is recorded, not guessed at.** If you block it, the chat
   stops being offered and the reason is kept — "he blocked us on Tuesday" is
   the answer to "why did the pager stop". Speaking to it again restores it,
   because being unblocked produces no event of its own.

component tested only against its own expectations is tested against nothing.**
