## What this asks for, separated out

Read as eight distinct capabilities, because they have different costs and
several can land independently:

1. **Understand instructions.** An agent must comprehend what it was asked,
   not pattern-match a job kind.
2. **Learn.** Something must persist across jobs and change later behaviour.
3. **Propose their own improvements.** An agent notices its own limitation and
   says what would fix it.
4. **Be aware of their role, constantly.** Not a system prompt read once — a
   standing sense of remit, and of the gap between remit and capability.
5. **Gain agency through additional skills and tools.** The route from "I could
   not do this" to "I can now" must exist, and be walkable by the agent.
6. **Ask Grant when they cannot do it themselves** — a proposal, with the
   reason, not a silent failure.
7. **Know the components they are dealing with.** Named explicitly: *what else
   they can do with the Google notebook, what they can create, what they can
   recover from it.* Also: *maybe marketing guys need access to particular
   tools like social media.*
8. **Be proactive, not reactive.** *"not just necessarily wait for me to tell
   them"* · *"to make suggestions, not just necessarily respond to specific
   questions"*.

## The framing

> *"create a unified project agentic space, team space"* — and
> *"we're just trying to make their own tool so that it's it's our leads"*.

Read alongside the standing goal in `CLAUDE.md`:

> *"I want to reduce my dependence on Claude or another coding interface for
> maintaining and extending the system."*

These are the same ambition. A capability that only exists because an agent in
a coding session built it is a capability the office does not have. The test
for every piece of this work: **can the office acquire this itself next time?**

## Explicit grant of autonomy

> *"I'd be open to letting you and, you know, work on that autonomously, but
> also give feedback on how that can be achieved and improvements you can make
> overall."*

Two deliverables, then: the work, and an assessment of how it should be done
and what else should change.
